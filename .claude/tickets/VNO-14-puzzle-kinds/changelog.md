# VNO-14 changelog (2026-10-22): more puzzle kinds

HUB-10 phase 3. One section per kind, in the order of Q5.

## 1. Bigger Murdle

- **Python** (`templates/puzzles/game/python-packages/vno_puzzles/`):
  - `generate("murdle", size=3|4, seed, band, motive=False, attrs=False)`. `motive` adds a fourth category (jealousy, greed ...) and `why` to the accusation; `attrs` makes some clues point at an item by a trait (`descr`: "the heavy weapon", "the tall guest") instead of its name, so the prose is about who or what, not which name. The default (3x3, no options) gives exactly the same puzzles as before (a fingerprint test).
  - `solver.py` rewritten for scale: `count_solutions` is a search that places the items of one category after another and checks a clue as soon as all the items it names are placed (the old enumeration stays as `count_solutions_brute`, the oracle of a test that compares the two on random clue sets); `deduce_fast` reaches the same fixed point as the recorded `deduce` with every cell a pass finds applied at once (the generator uses it while it grows and trims a clue set; only the final puzzle gets the recorded steps). 4x4 with a motive makes a puzzle in about 0.03 s.
  - `flavour.py` (was `verify.py`; renamed because `vno_puzzles.verify` is also a function): the read-back check compares a clue with its read-back by the assignments of the items they name only (so it scales), and understands the trait phrases of `descr`.
  - The solver and text already know the clue types of the logic-grid kind (`neither`, `oneof`, `before`, `after`, `apart`, the `order` rule, the ordinal category); they are used by commit 4.
- **TypeScript**: the puzzle core allows 3 or 4 categories, `descr`, a motive in the answer; `puzzleEntityIds`, `puzzleTextIds`, `puzzleFindableIds` (what needs a name, prose, a place in the story) replace the Murdle-only reads in `validateBook`; `vno_puzzle_generate` and `generate` of `vno_puzzle_set` take `size`, `motive`, `attrs`; `readBackTask` carries a legend ("the heavy weapon = rope") when clues use traits.
- **Pictures in the headers** (Q8): `links` of a puzzle entry (character, image, place) give the header a picture, the neutral sprite of a character or the insert picture of an object; the build marks those images as used, so they are made even when no beat shows them.
- **Ren'Py**: `vno_murdle` is now one generic staircase grid for any number of categories (4 items per category: 72 px cells; scrolls both ways inside the screen), with the clue list scrolling on the left, the accusation asks `Why?` for a motive, and the label also handles puzzles without a question (a logic grid is submitted as a grid; used by commit 4).
- **Tests**: Python (26 tests): 4x4 in every band (40 seeds), with a motive (30 seeds per band), with traits (30 seeds per band, the phrase is in the sentence and the name is not), search against brute force, trait read-back (a reader that mixes two traits is caught). TypeScript: the 4x4 fixture parses and validates in a book, the pictures and used images in the data file, the generate tool in under 2 s, `vno_puzzle_set` with `generate` of a 4x4 with a motive and traits, the legend in `readBackTask`. A `renpy-integration` case: a 4x4 with a motive, traits, two portraits and an object picture builds (the images are made) and lints clean. The self-test (`scripts/puzzle-poc.ts --selftest`) has a second case that opens a 4x4 puzzle.

## 2. Knights and Knaves

- **Python** (`knights.py`): speakers `k1..kn` (2 to 5), statements `{id, speaker, claim}`, a solution. Claims: `knight`, `knave`, `same`, `diff`, `some_knave`, `all_knight`, `exactly`, `not`, `and`, `or`. A knight's statement is true, a knave's false.
  - **Proofs**: exactly one of the 2^n assignments satisfies every statement (all are tried); and it can be found without guessing: `direct` (what every assignment that fits one statement agrees on is known) and `split` (assume a speaker is a knight, then a knave; the assumption that ends in a contradiction is ruled out). The band is the number of splits: easy 0, medium 1, hard 2 or more (so hard needs 3 or more speakers). Score: direct 1, split 4.
  - **Generator** (`generate("knights", size, seed, band)`): a random solution, the statements that are true for a knight and false for a knave (more kinds of claim for medium and hard, compound claims for hard), added until the assignment is unique, then every statement that is not needed is dropped; the result must be in its band. 200 seeds per band and per size (3, 4, 5) are all sound (1800 puzzles); 100 puzzles take 0.2 s.
  - **Text**: `Aldo says: "Brin and Dara are the same kind."`, "us" for everyone, an `intro` line. **Hints**: a wrong mark first, else the next step ("Suppose Aldo were a knight. ... So Aldo is a knave."). **Flavour check**: the reader writes each statement back as `{id, speaker, claim}`; each claim must be true in exactly the same assignments as the original (over all 2^n), then the read-back set must still be unique and found without guessing.
- **TypeScript**: `knightsCoreSchema` (a discriminated union with Murdle: `puzzleCoreSchema`), `puzzleEntityIds` / `puzzleTextIds` / `puzzleFindableIds` by kind, `readBackTask` per kind (`engine/puzzles/readback.ts`), `vno_puzzle_generate {kind: "knights"}`, `vno_puzzle_set {generate: {kind: "knights"}}`; the build writes the whole core as plain data, with the statements and names as translatable strings.
- **Ren'Py**: `screen vno_knights` (statements with speaker portraits, a Knight / Knave choice per speaker, Hint, Undo, Clear, Submit, Give up) and the same label for the loop, the tries, the hints and `onFail`.
- **Tests**: Python (12 tests) in `tests/puzzles/test_knights.py` (the runner of `tests/puzzles.test.ts` now runs every `test_*.py`); TS: the core and a book with a knights puzzle (`vn-puzzle-story`), the data and strings (`renpy-puzzle-emit`), the tools (`puzzle-python`), a `renpy-integration` case with portraits and an HR edition with translated statements (lint clean), and a third self-test case (`puzzle_smoke_knights`: marks a speaker, hint, submit).

## 3. Testimony

- **Python** (`testimony.py`): a core of `keys` (vocabulary: key, values, English templates), `statements` (`claims`, and `press.claims` said only when pressed), `evidence` (`facts`) and a `solution {statement, evidence, press}`. A claim and a fact clash when they are about the same key with different values; `solve` proves exactly one clash and that it is the pair the solution names (nothing to guess). Bands: easy (the clash is in the first words), medium (only after Press), hard (after Press, and 4 or more claims agree with evidence). `generate("testimony", None, seed, band)` draws from a built-in vocabulary of six keys (clock, door, lamp, weather, weapon, guest): 600 puzzles (200 seeds per band) are all sound and in band. An author can give their own keys, statements and evidence. Hints have four levels (what the evidence is about, press, the statement, the evidence). Names are unique (two reports are "weather report" and "weather report 2").
- **Flavour check**: the reader writes back the `claims` of each statement line (and the pressed line) and the `facts` of each piece of evidence; they must be the same sets, and the read-back puzzle must still have exactly one clash, the same one.
- **TypeScript**: `testimonyCoreSchema` in the union, `puzzleEntityIds` (witness `wit` and the evidence), `puzzleTextIds` (statements, `.press`, evidence, intro), `puzzleFindableIds` (the evidence: `clueAt` makes a piece appear in the record only after a beat), `readBackTask` for testimony, `vno_puzzle_generate {kind: "testimony"}` and `vno_puzzle_set`; the build writes the core as data, evidence found earlier has `at`.
- **Ren'Py**: `screen vno_testimony`: one statement at a time (Back, Next), Press (a second line, or "I have nothing to add"), the court record (evidence pictures from `links`, one button each), a penalty bar (5 places, or `onFail.maxTries`); a right pair that needs a press first says so and costs nothing; an empty bar loses the case, or in `retry` mode starts again after a recess. The label handles it with the same hints, tries and `onFail`.
- **Tests**: Python (12 tests, `tests/puzzles/test_testimony.py`), TS (core and book, the data and strings, the tools through the bridge incl. read-back and an edition), a `renpy-integration` case (witness portrait, an evidence picture, an HR edition; lint clean), the self-test `puzzle_smoke_testimony` (press, next, hint, a wrong present costs one).
